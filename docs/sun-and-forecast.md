# Sun, weather and terrain planner

The app has one selected UTC instant. The map control offers Now, a 15-minute elapsed-time slider, themed Pacific date, hour, minute, and AM/PM dropdowns with an explicit Apply time action, and sunset shortcuts for the selected viewpoint or the map center. Now advances every 30 seconds; pending edits remain stable while choosing a time, and a future selection remains fixed. Now and the slider reset pending edits. Invalid, past, out-of-window and skipped DST times show an error without locking the controls. Viewpoint details share the same selection. A photo leads the overview and progressively softens behind the translucent description panel as the user scrolls; reduced-motion users get a static photo. Map handoff links appear immediately below the photo and title. A single optional preferences sheet overlays the loaded map on first launch. Dismissible tips explain previews and sun symbols at those controls; the question-mark button opens the full three-step tutorial.

Overviews place the description and practical visit snapshot before the light planner. “Plan for the light” leads with a plain-language direction, sunset, golden hour and conditions. Missing weather, incomplete terrain and forecast age remain visible. Date/time editing and technical readings/methodology expand separately; routes, restrictions and source evidence remain in the expandable full visit guide. The snapshot uses a reported loop, round trip or explicitly labeled one-way route; it never presents a trail network length or summit elevation as walking effort.

Planner panels expand and fade, viewpoint content enters in a short stagger, and map cloud and solar-direction models crossfade between complete calculations rather than interpolating scientific values. The entire WebGL viewport is not opacity-animated: removing its retained launch fade resolved blank Safari rendering while preserving child transitions. Preferences → Transitions offers Smooth, Minimal, and Use device; the last choice follows the system reduced-motion preference. Dropdown closing releases pointer input immediately, so paused background-tab animations cannot leave controls blocked.

## Astronomy

Installed SunCalc **2.1.1** returns degrees clockwise from true north and apparent, already refracted solar altitude. `lib/solar.ts` uses that API directly. Event dates are explicitly selected in `America/Los_Angeles` using local noon and the correct Pacific offset. An evening selection never substitutes the next day's sunset. DST gaps are rejected. A repeated fall-back local time preserves the selected occurrence, or chooses the earliest occurrence still inside the forecast window; the UTC slider can reach either occurrence. Astronomy is calculated at the exact selected instant. Golden hour uses SunCalc's evening +6° crossing through sunset; civil twilight ends at its -6° crossing.

Independent fixtures from the [U.S. Naval Observatory](https://aa.usno.navy.mil/data/api) cover San Francisco in June, Berkeley in December, and San Jose on the spring DST change. Tests compare bearings/apparent altitude within 0.15° and rise/set/twilight events within 90 seconds of USNO's rounded-minute references. These tolerances are tests, not promises about observed atmospheric conditions. [SunCalc API](https://github.com/mourner/suncalc), [NOAA calculation assumptions](https://www.gml.noaa.gov/grad/solcalc/calcdetails.html).

## Offline terrain

`public/terrain/horizons.json` contains **279** prepared observation profiles from the 516-place catalog: **267 complete**, **12 partial**. **237** viewpoints are outside the first-release Bay Area preparation region. Uncovered bearings and places explicitly remain unavailable.

Ground heights come from native [USGS 3DEP getSamples](https://elevation.nationalmap.gov/arcgis/rest/services/3DEPElevation/ImageServer) at each exact catalog pin. The observer is 1.7 m above ground. Source raster IDs and sample dates are retained. Source resolution varies; the API's raw `nativeResolution` can be in metres or geographic degrees and is not mislabeled as universally 1 m. This build's source samples include 1 m and older geographic DEMs.

Surrounding terrain uses a bare F32 USGS 3DEP mosaic exported on a 0.001° grid (approximately 89 m east-west and 111 m north-south near the Bay; conservatively labeled ~112 m). Half-degree rays form conservative one-degree horizon bins. Radial sampling is 40 m out to 2 km, 80 m to 10 km, then 110 m to 60 km. Samples include geometric Earth curvature and no terrestrial refraction. SunCalc's apparent solar center receives only the +0.266° solar semidiameter; astronomical refraction is not applied twice. The crossing search runs from solar noon through standard astronomical sunset, refines transitions to five seconds internally, and retains reappearances through gaps. Displayed minute times are approximate. If already blocked at noon, no crossing time is invented.

Negative/missing offshore pixels are set to sea-surface zero only under the [Natural Earth public-domain ocean mask](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/about/terms-of-use/). Valid positive coastal ground is retained. Missing land remains missing. Remaining coastline, DEM age, coarse sampling, unresolved narrow ridges and exact standing-position errors can shift angles by several degrees and disappearance by many minutes. Native observer heights reduce summit smoothing bias, but do not refine the regional ray grid. Trees, buildings, railings, terrain beyond 60 km and later-than-standard-sunset horizon dip are not modeled. A complete profile means sample coverage, not verified angular accuracy.

USGS GNIS evidence corrected the East Peak pin, formerly about 612 m south of the summit. Three independent EPQS observer checks now agree with native ground heights within 0.3 m; independent near-ray spot checks expose coarse-grid angular uncertainty. Evidence is retained in `data/research/terrain-verification.json` and coordinate corrections. This is not exhaustive validation of every ray or field observation.

Rebuild without any runtime elevation calls:

```sh
python3 -m venv work/terrain/venv
work/terrain/venv/bin/pip install -r scripts/terrain-requirements.txt
work/terrain/venv/bin/python scripts/prepare-terrain.py
work/terrain/venv/bin/python scripts/verify-terrain.py
```

Raw downloads/cache remain under ignored `work/terrain`. Native ground requests use bounded batches of 40 and at most one retry. Failed preparation never produces invented profiles; the last successfully generated artifact remains until a complete file is written. Rebuild and revalidate when catalog coordinates change.

For additions inside the existing preparation region, `scripts/prepare-terrain.py --only <id> ...` can prepare selected profiles using the cached raster. It first checks that the raster, preparation settings and all retained pins match the previous artifact. The October 17 expansion added six complete coastal profiles using fresh native USGS observer samples; existing profiles and unavailable records were preserved.

## Weather and estimates

The fixed endpoint `/api/forecast` accepts no query parameters. It requests seven variables for 49 fixed Bay Area sample cells, 0.3° apart (roughly 26 × 33 km), from the free [Open-Meteo NOAA GFS/HRRR seamless API](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs/gfs-api). Coverage is latitude 36.8–38.9, longitude -123.2–-121.1. User coordinates are not sent to the provider.

The general API's default Best Match chooses applicable models automatically. For this feature we instead choose the coherent NOAA seamless product: HRRR where available with GFS for extended coverage. HRRR is nominally 3 km and commonly 18 hours (some runs 48 hours); GFS is roughly 13 km with longer coverage. The API does not identify the exact contributing model run for every hour, so the app labels the seamless source and does not invent per-hour run IDs or claim it is the most accurate option. No second paid source is used.

The service requests an hourly window extending past the next 48 hours plus a preceding hour. Cloud total/low/mid/high are percentages; visibility is metres; precipitation is the preceding-hour total in mm; `direct_radiation_instant` is instantaneous direct horizontal radiation in W/m², distinct from hourly radiation averages. Missing variables stay null. The nearest hourly value is used without extrapolation. Later forecast times are disabled if the available window is shorter. Neither a 15-minute slider nor smooth rendering implies 15-minute weather accuracy.

The cloud canvas uses 0–100% cloud cover to drive soft, translucent gray-white billows and wisps. Regional percentages blend between samples and fade at the edge of coverage; missing weather remains transparent. Cloud shapes are illustrative, not satellite imagery or predicted individual cloud positions. A deterministic world-anchored texture stays stable during pans, with cached low-resolution raster work capped at approximately 66,000 pixels. Opacity, visibility controls and model-to-model fades remain available. Detailed terrain screening only affects viewpoint markers/details. Colored sunlight shading has been removed; direct radiation still informs the selected-time weather description. The sun graphic uses a flat muted-gold disk and eight thin rounded rays, with a light outline for map contrast; muted rays and the status label distinguish blocked/below-horizon states. The direction pill uses a matching vector sun. Shared geometry positions hover and pinned previews clear of the sun, direction label and map pin, including entrance-animation clearance. Short viewports give the preview an internal scroll area when needed; the calculated sun bearing is never rotated to accommodate a card. Only the current sun fades in, so an outgoing symbol cannot linger behind a new preview. It is a direction indicator, not the sun's geographic position; the orange chevron points outward from the viewpoint toward the sun, and the separate purple dashed line shows sunset bearing. Map-pin hover previews a compact photo card and the corresponding direction without panning. Clicking pins the card and moves slightly closer; the direction follows the hovered or pinned viewpoint. A compact dark pill shows the true-north bearing, altitude and obstruction/night state.

Explicit scenery, search and area filters frame a compact group around the first recommendation without including a distant device location. The camera centers that actual viewpoint in the space between floating controls and cards. Empty results leave the current camera unchanged. Carousel measurements after a category reset do not trigger a second pan; subsequent carousel movement targets an actual visible card rather than the geographic midpoint between distant pins.

Viewing descriptions are deliberately heuristic: low clouds ≥75%, visibility <5 km, or precipitation ≥0.5 mm favor “Likely obscured”; moderate low/middle clouds, visibility <15 km or lighter precipitation favor “Mixed.” Required fields missing means “Weather unavailable.” High clouds alone do not veto a view. The sunset assessment also samples 30 km toward the sunset bearing where inside the grid. Cloud cover cannot reconstruct the three-dimensional sightline or predict sunset beauty. Direct radiation ≥100 W/m² only supports a more-favorable daytime sunlight description above 10° solar altitude; it is not divided by near-zero horizon radiation. No probability is fabricated.

## Cache, limits and hosting

Forecasts refresh on demand after **60 minutes** and reuse Cloudflare's shared Cache API when available. Concurrent requests are deduplicated within an isolate; a second isolate checks shared cached data before refreshing. Cache API storage is per data center, not a globally consistent database. Local development uses the same code and a process fallback. The client shares one window and never refetches when only time changes.

Each attempt reserves **49 location-equivalent calls**, not one merely because coordinates are batched. Seven variables and a short forecast window stay under the provider's normal single-location variable/time multiplier. Per-isolate caps are 98 equivalents/minute, 98/hour, 2,500/day, counting failures and retries. Normal sustained use at one cache location is about **1,176 equivalents/day**; cold starts, independent locations/isolates and retries can increase aggregate usage. There is no globally atomic quota guarantee with the existing unbound D1/R2 configuration. A global coordinator would be required for a strict deployment-wide budget at larger scale.

Provider attempts time out after 12 seconds, with at most one retry and 500 ms backoff for transient failures. HTTP 429 is not retried. Failures impose a ten-minute server cooldown. Cached weather is visibly aged and stops being used after **three hours**. Solar and available terrain remain independent.

[Open-Meteo's free service](https://open-meteo.com/en/pricing) is non-commercial, has no uptime guarantee, and currently lists 600/minute, 5,000/hour, 10,000/day and 300,000/month limits. The app includes Open-Meteo/NOAA attribution under CC BY 4.0 and labels its own derived assessments. No credentials, billing accounts, payment card, paid endpoint or new Google Maps dependency are introduced.

Existing Cloudflare configuration is preserved. Public [Workers limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/limits/) and [cache semantics](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/runtime-apis/cache/) were checked; the repository cannot establish the account's actual hosting plan or managed hosting allowance. Free operation is bounded by provider and host quotas, not unlimited.

Separate assertions preserve the solar bearing and expected above/below placement. Discovery tests also check filtered camera destinations, honest match reasons, strict scenery filtering and review evidence, complete route versus network distances, visitor-data provenance, outward solar arrows, optional setup, overview ordering, and visible uncertainty with technical disclosures closed. `npm run test`, `npm run typecheck`, `npm run build` are the checks for this release. Browser checks should cover time changes with stable map position, distinct selected-time/sunset sections, keyboard access, mobile controls, reduced motion and no extra forecast requests while scrubbing.

Native screenshots did not reliably reflect the current map/paint state, so they do not establish this revision's live camera or symbol appearance. An A/B check reproduced blank rendering with the retained root fade and confirmed normal map and overview rendering with Smooth enabled after its removal. Mobile layout of this revision remains unverified. Earlier forecast checks returned all 49 cells and 52 real hourly samples from the edge cache.
